# Digital Literacy — CSTA high school curriculum map

**Audience:** High school (primarily grades 9–10; stretch for 11–12)  
**Primary standards:** [CSTA K–12 Computer Science Standards, Revised 2017](https://csteachers.org/k12standards/) — **Level 3A** (core for all students)  
**Secondary:** ISTE Standards for Students 2024 (Digital Citizen, Knowledge Constructor, Creative Communicator)  
**Stretch:** Selected **Level 3B Impacts of Computing** where evaluation depth fits (equity, artifact impact)  
**Boundary with Cybersecurity:** This track stays at **citizenship / systems literacy / awareness**. Cyber owns deeper network defense, crypto, IR, and control justification (3A-NI / 3B-NI specialty depth).

CSTA does **not** define a separate “Digital Literacy” domain. For HS, digital literacy is the applied face of:

| CSTA concept | Role in this track |
| --- | --- |
| **Impacts of Computing (IC)** | Primary home — culture, equity, collaboration, IP, privacy, ethics |
| **Computing Systems (CS)** | Abstraction layers, OS roles, systematic troubleshooting |
| **Networks & the Internet (NI)** | How networks work + everyday security/privacy tradeoffs (awareness) |
| **Data & Analysis (DA)** | Storage/organization tradeoffs; light representation awareness |

Level **3A** is the design target (ages ~14–16, all students). Level **3B IC** appears as stretch evaluation in Weeks 4–5 and the capstone — not as AP CS specialty programming.

## 8-week arc (HS)

| Week | Theme | Focus |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Computing Systems Foundations | Abstraction, hardware/software/OS layers, data storage tradeoffs |
| 2 | Networks & Information Integrity | How networks move data; search fluency; evaluate claims in a computing culture |
| 3 | Connected Communication & Work | Professional digital communication; collaboration tools across cultures/careers |
| 4 | Identity, Culture & Equity | Footprint/reputation; evaluate computing’s cultural impacts and equity deficits |
| 5 | Creation, Accessibility & IP | Build artifacts that reduce bias/access barriers; IP law benefits *and* harms |
| 6 | Privacy, Security & Tradeoffs | Everyday threats + recommend measures; automated collection; privacy ethics |
| 7 | Agency: Wellbeing & Troubleshooting | Personal impacts of computing; systematic troubleshooting others can reuse |
| 8 | Futures & Capstone | Careers/connected work; evaluate impacts and ship a personal action plan |

## Design rules for lesson writers

1. **Tone:** High school (college apps, first jobs, internships, scholarships, group projects) — not middle-school “be nice online” alone.
2. **Verbs match CSTA:** *evaluate, compare, recommend, explain tradeoffs, develop guidelines, test/refine* — not only *identify* or *list*.
3. **No exploit content:** Threat lessons stay recognition + response (same boundary as Cyber literacy).
4. **Students don’t see standard codes** in slides; codes live in this doc / instructor crosswalk only.
5. **Overlap with Cyber:** dl-11/12 stay habit + tradeoff focused; point advanced students to Cybersecurity for architecture/IR depth.
6. **Target length:** ~18–25 interactive slides per lesson (existing module shape), with check-ins that force evaluation, not recall-only.
